## Route A — Water Research Critical Review

This route keeps the manuscript's current self-description (a critical review synthesizing WBE's measurement-inference literature) and completes the evidentiary work that description obligates.

**Must complete before this route reaches submission:**

- **Formal database search.** The existing core-plus-seven-complementary-module Boolean strategy (`WBE_Review_Systematic_Search_Strategy.md`) must actually be run against Web of Science, Scopus, and PubMed — not merely designed. Zero searches have been executed to date; `systematic-review/`'s toolkit is built and tested only against synthetic data.
- **Full text obtained and read.** At minimum, the 21 references currently cited in the condensed draft (`WBE_Review_Condensed_References_Working.md`); in practice, most of the 67 active references, since a critical review's claims are expected to rest on the reviewer's own reading, not abstract summaries. Current state: 0/67 obtained, 0/67 read.
- **9 Critical + 7 High claim verification.** Every row in `WBE_Review_Critical_Claim_Verification_Queue.csv` must reach a `support_outcome` other than "Unable to verify" for the manuscript to responsibly retain its current claim strength; rows that cannot be resolved must be narrowed, qualified, or removed per that file's `decision_if_unresolved` field. Current state: 0/9 resolved.
- **Table 2 evidence threshold.** Every cell needs a defensible answer to "direct comparison evidence, single-scenario observation, mechanistic inference, or author synthesis?" (per this project's own evidence-tier discipline) before it can be presented as a synthesis table rather than a labeled proposal. Currently every cell is abstract-checked only.
- **Table 3 evidence threshold.** Every "minimum validation" and "principal failure mode" cell needs confirmation against the underlying study's actual validation design (not internal fit, R², or RMSE alone) — this is exactly the kind of check Phase 3D's stalled full-text acquisition was meant to perform.
- **PRISMA / Evidence Freeze.** If this route retains any framing that implies systematic coverage (even the current, honestly-hedged "narrative synthesis... establishes a defensible starting evidence set" framing in §1.2), reviewers at a journal like Water Research may reasonably expect PRISMA-ScR-conformant search documentation. This route either commits to producing that documentation for real, or must state even more explicitly than the current draft that it is not attempting systematic coverage — the latter starts to blur into Route B.
- **Still missing before actual submission, even after full-text verification completes:** a real Abstract, Highlights, and cover letter (only structural outlines currently exist); the SI §S4 existing-review competition matrix (flagged in `WBE_Review_Supplementary_Outline.md` as new content, not yet drafted); final reference renumbering (currently deferred by design); Table 1 and Table 4, which are planned but never drafted (`WBE_Review_Table_Design_Brief.md`); and the four figures, which are specified (`WBE_Review_Figure_Design_Brief.md`) but not produced as images.
- **Preserving the four original contributions under this route:** the four propositions (§1.3 — inverse-problem framing, amplitude-temporal-spatial taxonomy, normalization-as-mechanism-specific-correction, identifiability/uncertainty-aware framework) can remain the manuscript's throughline, but every specific empirical example currently illustrating them (Rainey/Darling, Janssens, Zhang, Dai, Link/Garrido, etc.) must individually clear full-text verification before continuing to appear as evidence for them. The propositions themselves do not require external verification (they are explicitly labeled as this review's own synthesis), but a critical review's readers will expect the illustrating evidence to be solid, not merely plausible.

## Route B — Conceptual Perspective/Framework Article

This route repositions the manuscript as an argued conceptual framework illustrated by examples, rather than a critical synthesis of the field's evidence base — the same four propositions, without the implicit promise of evidentiary coverage a "critical review" carries.

**Must remove or change:**

- **"Systematic review" adjacent language to delete.** §1.2's entire PRISMA-ScR sentence ("a defensible scoping review of this literature would follow PRISMA-ScR... report a PRISMA flow diagram, none of which has been performed at this stage") becomes unnecessary and slightly confusing under this route — a perspective piece does not need to explain why it isn't a scoping review, because no reader would expect it to be one. This sentence should be deleted, not merely re-hedged.
- **Comprehensiveness/coverage claims to delete.** "This approach establishes a defensible starting evidence set" (§1.2) implies an evidence-gathering exercise with a completion criterion; under Route B this becomes "the examples below illustrate, but do not exhaustively survey, the literature." Similarly, phrases like "the evidence base shows a consistent pattern" (§4.3, on biomarker validation failures) and "across independent multi-site validation studies" (§4.3) assert a literature-wide pattern that a perspective piece is not positioned to claim; these need "several studies illustrate..." framing instead.
- **Empirical comparisons to narrow.** Every comparative claim currently framed as a finding about the field — flow-vs-biomarker normalization performance (§3.5, §4.2, citing Rainey et al. and Darling et al. together as if jointly establishing a context-dependent pattern), the four-system data-fusion claim (§6.2, Zhang et al.), the passive-sampler spatial-scale claim (§7.1, Schang et al.) — needs to shift from "the literature shows X" to "one study illustrates X; whether this generalizes is not established here." This is a framing change, not necessarily a deletion, but it changes almost every citation's surrounding sentence.
- **Table 2 and Table 3 → explicit conceptual-framework tables.** Both tables already carry a "synthesized by this review" caption (added Phase 3C), which is a good start but not sufficient under Route B — the caption should go further, to something like "a proposed classification, illustrated with single-study examples not intended as a comprehensive evidence synthesis," and any cell phrased as reporting a finding (e.g., Table 2's "Residual/introduced uncertainty" column, which currently states outcomes as if established) needs to be explicitly framed as this review's own risk-assessment, not a literature-derived conclusion.
- **Quantitative figures and cross-scenario conclusions to delete.** Any number tied to a single non-full-text-verified study should be removed outright rather than hedged further — this includes the qualitative-but-still-comparative claims like "flow normalization outperforming biomarker alternatives in some large multi-site studies... while becoming unreliable under substantial inflow and infiltration in others" (§3.5, §4.2), which is already appropriately hedged for Route A but is still a stronger empirical claim than Route B should make without full-text verification. Under Route B, the safer version states the *conceptual* point (normalization validity is condition-dependent) without asserting which specific conditions favor which specific method, since that assertion is exactly what needs full-text confirmation.
- **Title, abstract structure, and section structure.** The current title ("Beyond Detection in Wastewater-Based Epidemiology: Sewer Processes, Signal Distortion, Normalization, and Epidemiological Inference") is largely compatible with either route, but could be sharpened toward Route B with an explicit framework/perspective marker, e.g. "...: A Conceptual Framework for Signal Distortion and Epidemiological Inference" or "...: Toward an Inverse-Problem Framework." An Abstract under Route B would drop any sentence resembling a coverage claim (compare `WBE_Review_Abstract_Outline.md` slot 3, "scope sentence," which currently must disclose the non-PRISMA-compliant search process — under Route B this disclosure becomes moot, since no reader expects search-based coverage from a perspective piece, and the Abstract can spend that space on the propositions instead). Section structure could compress: Parts 2-4's evidence-heavy subsections (source-state variation, sewer transport mechanisms, normalization) could be shortened once they no longer need to carry synthesis weight, potentially freeing word budget for a more developed treatment of the identifiability/uncertainty framework (Part 5) and the readiness/decision framework (Part 7), which are this review's most original material.
- **Possibly more suitable venues/article types.** If Water Research does not have a "Perspective" or "Opinion" category distinct from "Critical Review," comparable venues that regularly publish conceptual-framework pieces in this space include journals with an explicit Perspective/Viewpoint track (e.g., environmental-science journals' "Perspective" sections) or a methodology-and-commentary-oriented outlet. **This is a suggestion to investigate, not a verified fact about any specific journal's current editorial policy** — confirming which journal/article-type combination actually fits would itself require checking current author guidelines, which is outside this memo's scope and not something to assert without verification.
